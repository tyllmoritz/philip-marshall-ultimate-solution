    How it works

    1. The Cross — four top edges, found with a short search.
    2. 3 center section edge pieces / 3 F2L pairs — the Edge Series (Hedgeslammer F R' F' R, inverse: Sledgehammer) cycles three edges; setup turns bring pieces into position and are undone afterwards. With Partial F2L on, three corner–edge pairs are inserted next to the cross instead (using standard F2L algorithms, or the series if that is shorter), so step 3 only has to finish the 5 remaining edges without disturbing them.
    3. Top edge pieces — series (with setup) until the key position: one middle-layer edge and two last-layer edges are wrong and a single Hedgeslammer / Sledgehammer / sexy move solves them. An aligning U turn may end this step.
    4. Last three edge pieces — that one series.
    5. Five corner pieces — until the last 2–3 corners share one side; the Corner Series (Niklas, U R U' L' U R' U' L and its mirrors/rotations) cycles three corners without touching any edge.
    6. The End Game — the last three corners fall into place together.

    Method and name: Philip Marshall's solution. At each step the solver builds several candidate solutions (series with setup that place more pieces without disturbing finished ones) and keeps the one with the lowest movecount coefficient (MCC). It is an independent implementation, so move counts differ from the original write-up. Movecount coefficient by Trangium (MIT). Cube rendering by cubing.js. Smart cube support uses smartcube-web-bluetooth (Pau Oliva, MIT; a fork of gan-web-bluetooth by Andy Fedotov) and takes ideas from Cubedex.
